One row per positioned, layered, clipped, rounded, or shape-wrapped element.
Element Content purpose Normal-flow baseline Chosen technique and why Containing block / stacking context Fallback or disengagement Required test
.skip-link Keyboard users can skip past the header and story guide straight to the article. It's the first link on the page, so with no styling it just shows up as a normal link at the top. I will use position:absolute to keep it off-screen until it gets :focus-visible, then it will show at the top-left. Because it's absolute, showing it doesn't push anything else around. No positioned parent, so it uses the initial containing block. I will give it z-index:var(--layer-overlay), which is the top layer. Tha makes its own stacking context and keeps the sticky guide from covering it. With CSS turned off it's still a visible link at the top of the page. Tabbing in from the address bar at 320px, 1280px and 200% zoom. The link should show up, not be covered, and move focus to the article.
.issue-badge A small "1940s - 1960s" tag that dates the story at a glance. The lede will say the same thing, so nothing is lost if the badge is missed. With no styling it's just a line of text right after the lede. I will use position:absolute with inset-block-start and inset-inline-end to pin it to the header's top corner no matter how long the title is. I will add inline-end padding to the header so the title never runs under it. The header will be position:relative, so it's the containing block. I won't give the badge a z-index, so it doesn't make a new stacking context. It will still paint above the header text because it's positioned. Under 52rem I will turn the positioning off and it will sit in normal flow under the lede. I will check 320px, 768px, and 1280px, long title, and 200% zoom. The badge should never overlap the eyebrow or the title.
